Benefits and financial support if you're disabled or have a health condition
Find financial and practical support if a disability or health condition affects your daily life or ability to work.
Contents
Overview
Check what you could get
Benefits and financial support
Help to find or stay in work
Other support
Overview
Some support is based on how your condition affects daily living or mobility. Other support depends on your ability to work, income, savings, age, or National Insurance record.
You can check eligibility even if you work or do not have a formal diagnosis. Each payment has its own rules.
Check what you could get
Use an eligibility checker or benefits calculator to identify possible support. You may need information about your health, care needs, income, savings, housing, and employment.
Special claim arrangements may be available for people nearing the end of life.
Benefits and financial support
An assessment may consider how your condition affects you rather than the name of the condition alone. Provide relevant examples and supporting evidence.
Personal Independence Payment can help with extra daily living or mobility costs.
Attendance Allowance can help with care needs after the qualifying age.
Disability Living Allowance may be available for a disabled child.
Employment and Support Allowance or Universal Credit may help when a condition affects your ability to work.
Help to find or stay in work
Workplace support may pay for practical help, specialist equipment, travel, or communication assistance at an interview. It does not replace an employer's duty to make reasonable adjustments.
Discuss adjustments with your employer and check what evidence an employment-support scheme requires.
Other support
Other help may include home adaptations, support for disabled students or children, tax allowances for blind people, and compensation for certain injuries or conditions.
A support visit may be available if you cannot manage a claim by phone, online, or in writing.

Other support
Overview
Support for families can help with time away from work, everyday costs, childcare, education, and the additional needs of a disabled child.
Eligibility can depend on your income, employment, household, the age of your children, and where you live.
Check what you could get
Use a benefits calculator or eligibility checker with details of your household, earnings, savings, housing costs, and childcare costs.
Check whether a payment affects other benefits or is subject to an income charge before you apply.
Time off and pay when you have or adopt a child
You may qualify for maternity, paternity, adoption, shared parental, or neonatal care leave and pay. Some people who do not qualify for statutory maternity pay may qualify for a separate allowance.
Tell your employer within the required time and keep evidence of expected birth, adoption, or placement dates.
Child Benefit
Child Benefit can provide regular support for a child. The claim may also protect National Insurance credits for a parent or carer who is not working.
Higher-income households may need to pay a tax charge. Review the claim when a child turns 16 or their education or training changes.
Help with childcare costs
Depending on your circumstances, help may include funded childcare, tax-free childcare, support through Universal Credit, or grants for students and young parents.
Check which schemes can be used together and whether your childcare provider must be registered or approved.
Other support
Help with school meals or healthy food may be available for qualifying families.
Separated parents can use child maintenance services to arrange financial support.
Disabled children and their families may qualify for disability benefits, grants, or home adaptations.
A support visit may be available if you need help making a benefit claim.

Benefits and financial support if you're caring for someone
Find financial help and practical support if you regularly care for someone who is disabled, ill, or needs assistance.
Contents
Overview
Check what you could get
Benefits and financial support
Managing benefits for someone else
Other support
Overview
You may be able to get support if you spend significant time caring for someone. Your entitlement can depend on the care you provide, your earnings, and the benefits received by the person you care for.
A carer's assessment from your local authority can identify practical support even if you do not qualify for a payment.
Check what you could get
Use a benefits calculator with details of your income, savings, household, working hours, and caring responsibilities.
Before claiming, check whether a carer payment could affect a severe disability addition or another payment received by the person you care for.
Benefits and financial support
Carer's Allowance may be available if you provide enough care and meet the earnings rules.
Carer's Credit can protect your National Insurance record if caring limits your ability to work.
Universal Credit may include additional support for caring responsibilities.
Pension Credit may include an extra amount for eligible carers.
Managing benefits for someone else
If a person cannot manage their own benefit claim, you can ask to become their appointee. This makes you responsible for the claim, reporting changes, and using payments in their interests.
Becoming an appointee is different from having legal authority over the person's wider finances.
Other support
Contact the relevant benefit office if you need help with a claim. A support visit may be available when the usual phone, online, or written routes are not suitable.
The person you care for may also qualify for a disability or attendance benefit based on their own needs.

for people on a low income, a budgeting loan, or an arranged support
visit if you cannot manage a claim through the usual channels.

Contents
Overview
Check what you could get
Help with housing costs
Help with heating costs
Help if you're over the qualifying pension age
Other support
Overview
Support may be available whether you are working, looking for work, unable to work, or over the qualifying pension age.
What you can receive depends on your income, savings, household, housing costs, health, and where you live.
Check what you could get
Use a benefits calculator or eligibility checker with recent information about your earnings, savings, rent or mortgage, Council Tax, and household.
Check each scheme separately because local and national support can use different rules.
Help with housing costs

Your local authority may offer Council Tax Reduction or additional help when your housing support does not cover all of your rent.
Help with heating costs
Some households can receive a discount or seasonal payment towards energy costs. Eligibility can depend on age, income, benefits, energy supplier, or local weather conditions.
Check whether support is automatic or requires an application, and keep your energy account details available.
Help if you're over the qualifying pension age
Pension Credit can top up a low retirement income and may provide access to other help. Additional support can include winter payments and a free or discounted television licence for eligible people.
A claim may be possible even if you own your home or have some savings.
Other support
Local authorities may provide help with essential living costs.
Help may be available with prescriptions and other health costs.
A savings scheme or budgeting loan may be available to some benefit recipients.
Benefit deductions can sometimes be arranged to repay priority debts and bills.
A support visit may be available if you need help making a claim.

Guidance
Manage an existing benefit, payment or claim
Find out how to use your online account, receive payments, report
changes, challenge a decision, and deal with overpayments.
Contents
Overview
Manage your claim online
Payments
Report a change
Challenge a decision
Overpayments and fraud
Overview
The way you manage a benefit depends on the benefit you receive.
Keep your account details and award notices available when you
contact the responsible office.
Report changes promptly. A delay can affect your payments or leave
you owing money that must be repaid.
Manage your claim online
Online accounts may be available for Universal Credit, childcare
support, and Help to Save. You may also be able to send evidence,
such as a fit note, through an online service.
Use the account named on your latest letter or decision notice. Do
not send passwords or security codes by email.
Payments
Payment dates and frequency vary by benefit. Your award notice or
online account should show when and how you will be paid.
You may be able to request an advance before a first payment.
Alternative arrangements may be available if you cannot use a bank account.
Deductions can be made from benefits to repay debts or bills.
Some people may qualify for seasonal or disability-related payments.
Report a change
Tell the relevant benefit office when your income, address,
household, caring responsibilities, or other circumstances change.
You should also report extended travel, a hospital stay, or a move
into residential care when these may affect your claim.
Check your benefit letter or online account for the correct way to
report the change.
Challenge a decision
If you think a decision is wrong, first read the decision notice.
It explains whether you can ask for a mandatory reconsideration and
the deadline for doing so.
After a reconsideration, you may be able to appeal to an independent
tribunal. Keep copies of your decision notices and supporting
evidence.
Overpayments and fraud
Contact the office named on an overpayment notice if you do not
understand the amount, need to arrange repayment, or believe the
decision is incorrect.
Suspected benefit fraud can be reported separately. Only provide
information you know to be accurate.

Find financial help if illness or a health condition means you cannot work for a limited period.
Contents
Overview
Check what you could get
Benefits and financial support
Evidence and changes
Overview
You may be able to get financial support when you are temporarily unable to work. The support available depends on your employment status, earnings, savings, household, and how long your illness lasts.
Tell your employer or the relevant benefit service when your circumstances change, including when you return to work.
Check what you could get
A benefits calculator can estimate the support you may receive. An eligibility checker can also help you understand which claims to consider before you apply.
You may need details of your income, savings, housing costs, household, and recent employment. An estimate is not an award decision.
Benefits and financial support
Different payments cover different circumstances. Check the eligibility rules and whether your employer must pay you before starting a benefit claim.
Statutory Sick Pay may be available from your employer for a qualifying period of illness.
Employment and Support Allowance may be available if illness or disability affects how much you can work.
Universal Credit may help with living and housing costs if your income is reduced.
Evidence and changes
You may be asked for medical evidence, such as a fit note, and information about your work and earnings. Keep copies of anything you submit.
Report changes promptly, including changes to your health, income, household, or ability to work. A delay can affect your payment or create an overpayment.

Find out what you need to do when you hire, pay and manage staff.
Contents
Overview
Recruit staff
Make required checks
Pay your employees
Meet workplace duties
Overview
Employers have legal duties from the time they recruit staff. These include fair recruitment, record keeping, pay, tax, workplace safety and employment rights.
Recruit staff
Write a clear job description and use a fair selection process. Give successful applicants a written statement of their main terms of employment.
Make required checks
Check that each worker has the right to work before employment begins. You may also need background checks or professional registration for particular roles.
Pay your employees
Register as an employer where required and operate payroll. Pay at least the applicable minimum wage, give payslips and report pay and deductions through the appropriate service.
Meet workplace duties
Provide a safe workplace, consider pension duties and respect rights to leave, rest and fair treatment. Keep employment records and deal with concerns promptly.

Plan your business
